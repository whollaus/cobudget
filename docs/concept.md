# The CoBudget approach

## Focus: your household, alone and together

CoBudget helps you understand everyday income and expenses, plan upcoming costs
and share expenses fairly. It brings your personal household book and shared
areas together: a shared purchase appears as your own share in your personal
finances, while the area keeps the total, who paid and the settlement history.

This works for individuals, couples, families, housemates and small groups. You
can keep track of your household, organize a trip or separate another part of
your everyday finances without setting up a bank-account structure first.

## Organized around payments and people

The starting point is a payment: what it was for, when it belongs in your budget,
its amount and, for shared costs, who paid and how it is divided. Categories,
payment partners, labels and tags help you organize and understand those records.
Areas group payments by purpose; workspaces separate independent sets of data.

Your personal reports and budget goals use your own amounts, including your share
of shared expenses. A shared area also shows the total and the members' outstanding
balances. Those balances describe what members owe each other; they are not bank
account balances.

For example, if you pay €120 for a shared purchase and split it equally with one
other person, the area records the €120 purchase and you as the payer. Each
person's personal overview includes €60 of expenses. With no other open payments,
the other person owes you €60. This keeps your share of the cost separate from
the amount that left your bank account.

## Bank accounts and synchronization

CoBudget does not connect to bank accounts, synchronize bank transactions or
import bank statements. It does not maintain reconciled bank account balances.
You record income and expenses yourself; recurring rules can create further
payment records inside CoBudget.

Reports therefore describe the payments recorded in CoBudget and their allocation.
Their income-minus-expenses balance is a budget overview, not a live bank balance.
Planned payments, reminders and settlements help you organize what is due;
CoBudget does not initiate bank transfers or move money.

CSV exports let you use your records in a spreadsheet. CoBudget backup imports
restore app data; they are separate from importing transactions from a bank.
